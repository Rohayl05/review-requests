==================================================
1. BUSINESS PROBLEM
==================================================

Business customers provide one or a small handful of PDF documents describing:

- authorized payment signatories;
- non-signatories who may initiate, verify or participate in workflows;
- signing groups, classes or roles;
- whether a person may sign singly or jointly;
- monetary authorization limits;
- which other groups or named people they may sign with;
- payment or account restrictions;
- amendments to earlier mandates.

The documents may be board resolutions, authorized signatory lists, account mandates, amendments or similar business documents.

The existing process is manual or semi-manual. A user reads the PDFs, identifies the signatories and rules, assigns normalized groups such as A, B, C and D, derives valid payment approval combinations, and enters the result into Excel.

The goal is to automate most of this work while retaining human review.

The output may eventually be entered into a digital banking system. Therefore this proof of concept must prioritize:

- correctness;
- traceability;
- explainability;
- deterministic validation;
- human approval;
- no silent assumptions.

This is not just a PDF table extraction project. It is a document-to-authorization-rules pipeline.

5. GROUPING POLICY
==================================================

There are two grouping modes.

MODE 1: GROUPS PROVIDED BY THE CUSTOMER DOCUMENT

If the documents explicitly define lettered groups such as A, B, C or D:

- preserve those group labels;
- preserve the customer-provided group membership;
- preserve the associated rules;
- do not rename the groups;
- do not silently reorder them;
- flag internal inconsistencies for review.

MODE 2: GROUPS NOT PROVIDED

If the documents describe people, job roles or authorities but do not provide usable letter groups:

- create groups dynamically;
- Group A should normally represent the strongest authority class;
- Group B should represent the next distinct authority class;
- continue with C, D, E and so on;
- assign the same group only to participants with operationally equivalent authority;
- store an explanation for every derived group assignment.

Do not assign groups based only on job titles.

Do not assign groups based only on the order in which people appear.

Create a structured authority profile for each participant before grouping.

An authority profile may include:

- participant type;
- allowed action;
- single signing limit;
- joint signing limit;
- unlimited status;
- required partner groups;
- required named partners;
- prohibited partners;
- number of required signers;
- payment scope;
- account scope;
- special conditions.

Suggested authority ordering considerations:

1. A signatory generally ranks above an initiate-only or verify-only participant.
2. For otherwise equivalent authority, single signing is generally broader than joint-only signing.
3. Unlimited authority is generally broader than bounded authority.
4. A higher amount limit is generally broader than a lower limit.
5. Fewer partner restrictions generally represent broader authority.
6. General payment authority is broader than authority restricted to one payment or account type.

Do not collapse this into one unexplained numeric score.

Some authority profiles may be different without being strictly comparable.

Example:

- Person X may sign singly up to AED 100,000.
- Person Y may sign jointly up to AED 10,000,000.

These people should usually be separate authority classes. The program should document the chosen letter ordering and send ambiguous ordering to review.

Most importantly:

All members of one normalized group must behave equivalently in the generated workflow model.

7. SIGNING RULE MODEL
==================================================

Support:

- single signer authority;
- joint authority;
- group-based partner requirements;
- named-person partner requirements;
- multiple required signers from one group;
- mixed group combinations;
- bounded amounts;
- unlimited amounts;
- minimum and maximum amount ranges;
- non-signatory plus signatory workflows;
- payment-specific restrictions;
- account-specific restrictions;
- explicitly prohibited combinations;
- unclear or unsupported rules.

Example normalized workflows:

- 1A
- 2A
- 1A + 1B
- 2C
- 1E + 1C + 1B

The code means:

- 1A = one member of Group A
- 2B = two distinct members of Group B
- 1A + 1C = one member of A and one distinct member of C

Do not assume the same individual may satisfy two positions in one workflow.

Store workflow components structurally. The formatted code such as "1A+1B" must be a derived display value, not the primary data structure.

Example:

{
  "components": [
    {"group_id": "A", "count": 1},
    {"group_id": "B", "count": 1}
  ]
}

Named-person restrictions must not be generalized to an entire group.

Example:

If the source states:

"Ahmed must sign jointly with Fatima"

do not convert that to:

"1A + 1B"

unless every valid A and B member combination is genuinely permitted.

The normalized workflow must be capable of retaining named participant constraints.

13. VALIDATION ENGINE
==================================================

Implement validation as deterministic Python rules.

Use severity levels:

- INFO
- WARNING
- ERROR
- BLOCKING

At minimum validate:

1. Every derived rule has source evidence.
2. Every workflow references existing rules.
3. Every participant referenced by a rule exists.
4. Every group referenced by a workflow exists.
5. Every group has at least one member.
6. A participant is not duplicated within one workflow.
7. Two required signer positions cannot be fulfilled by one person.
8. Maximum amount is not lower than minimum amount.
9. Unlimited is represented consistently.
10. Joint signing requires at least two distinct signing positions.
11. A source statement saying "singly" is not converted only into a joint workflow.
12. A named-person restriction is not generalized to the whole group.
13. An exclusively non-signatory participant cannot authorize alone.
14. Workflow ranges do not conflict unexpectedly.
15. Detect amount-range gaps.
16. Detect amount-range overlaps.
17. Detect duplicate workflows.
18. Detect contradictory participant authorities.
19. Detect an unsupported currency.
20. Detect rules with missing or low-quality evidence.
21. Detect unresolved duplicate-person candidates.
22. Detect unresolved cross-document conflicts.
23. Detect groups whose members have non-equivalent authority signatures.
24. Detect an AI-generated person not found in evidence.
25. Detect an AI-generated monetary amount not present in or clearly derived from evidence.
26. Block approval if any BLOCKING issue remains unresolved.

Do not assume every overlap is invalid.

Some mandates may intentionally allow multiple valid approval combinations for the same amount.

Classify overlaps as:

- equivalent duplicate;
- valid alternative workflow;
- possible conflict;
- definite conflict.

If the system cannot determine which one applies, send it to review.
